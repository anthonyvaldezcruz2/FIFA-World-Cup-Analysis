# Figure 1: World Cup Championship Wins
champion_counts = df['CHAMPION'].value_counts()
champion_counts = champion_counts[champion_counts > 1]

colors = ['gold', 'steelblue', 'tomato', 'mediumseagreen', 'mediumpurple']

fig, (ax1, ax2) = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(10, 4))

ax1.bar(champion_counts.index, champion_counts.values, color=colors)
ax1.set_title('Wins by Country')
ax1.set_ylabel('Wins')

ax2.pie(champion_counts.values, labels=champion_counts.index, colors=colors, autopct='%1.1f%%')
ax2.set_title('Share of Titles')

plt.suptitle('Fig 1: World Cup Championship Wins', fontweight='bold')
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

#Figure 5: Correlation Between Teams and Goals

def host_result(row):
    if row['HOST'] == row['CHAMPION']:
        return '1st Place'
    elif row['HOST'] == row['RUNNER UP']:
        return '2nd Place'
    elif row['HOST'] == row['THIRD PLACE']:
        return '3rd Place'
    else:
        return 'Did Not Place'

df['HOST RESULT'] = df.apply(host_result, axis=1)

result_counts = df['HOST RESULT'].value_counts()

colors = ['tomato', 'steelblue', 'gold', 'mediumseagreen']

fig, (ax1, ax2) = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(10, 4))

ax1.bar(result_counts.index, result_counts.values, color=colors)
ax1.set_title('Host Country Results')
ax1.set_ylabel('Count')

ax2.pie(result_counts.values, labels=result_counts.index, colors=colors, autopct='%1.1f%%')
ax2.set_title('Share of Host Results')

plt.suptitle('Fig 5: Did Hosting Give an Advantage?', fontweight='bold')
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

Fig 5: Host country performance across all tournaments, showing that while most hosts did not place, a notable number won or finished on the podium.

Actionable Advice:

Based on my analysis of the FIFA World Cup data from 1930 to 2022, I have three recommendations. First, FIFA should continue expanding the tournament, the data clearly shows that every time we increased the number of teams (1982 and 1998), the number of matches and total goals scored went up significantly, meaning more games and more excitement for fans. Second, since average goals per game have steadily declined since 1954, I recommend FIFA look into rule changes or format adjustments that could encourage more attacking play and bring scoring back up. However, this could also be a result of defensive play improving significantlly. Third, I recommend we start collecting attendance and viewership data for each match. Right now the dataset only tells us what happened on the field, but if we had audience data we could identify which matchups and which rounds drive the most interest, helping us make smarter decisions around scheduling and marketing for future tournaments.

With the 2026 World Cup coming to North America, hosted across the United States, Canada, and Mexico, this will be the largest World Cup in history, featuring 48 teams for the first time. This is a massive opportunity for FIFA, and the trends in our data back it up. Expanding to 48 teams means more matches, more goals, and a bigger global audience than ever before. North America is one of the largest sports markets in the world, and bringing the tournament here gives us a chance to grow the sport in a region where soccer is still gaining popularity.
